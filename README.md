<summary>🐌 Click for the long-winded "Peter Parker to Spiderman" origin story </summary>
<br>

When I read **Sapiens** during the COVID-19 pandemic of 2020, I was amused by Yuval Noah Harari's claim that humanity's perception of death had shifted from "a morbid inevitability" to "a technical problem" that must be solved. I suppose, if somebody did solve the problem of death and committed all their work onto github, it would end up being the repository with the highest pull and fork requests of all time! Fortunately or unfortunately so, both you and I are miles and lightyears behind that accomplishment, so good luck to the both of us for our contributions. But let's not forget, it took about **65000 years** for humans to go from wall paintings to papyrus scrolls, **3500 years** to go from papyrus scroll to Gutenberg's printing press, and only about **500 years** from the printing press to the e-mail. So within our lifetimes, somebody might very well solve this problem of death and we will be the stupid ones who laughed at that idea. 😝

Personally, I don't yet see myself to be that proficient in my work yet, but my curiosity about the ailments of the human body is what brought me to this fascinating intersection of machine learning, data engineering, and biology to build tools that turn messy biomedical data into real insight. I'm a **Bioinformatics graduate** from **Georgia Institute of Technology**, and before grad school, I spent three years as a consultant at **IQVIA**, driving predictive modeling and analytics engagements for Fortune 100 pharmaceutical clients across the Asia Pacific, the Middle East, and Europe.

Before you ask me what Bioinformatics is, I'll crisply break it down into the phrase - **Data Science for biological research**. The same CLI and programming tools as your average Software Developer, the same tech stacks and other weird buzzwords like **end-to-end ML pipeline** as your average Data Scientist, the same cloud platforms and ETL pipelines like your average Data Engineer and Analyst; but a whole other universe of varied datasets from the rich expanse of biology. Text data formats like csv, structured tabular data, unstructired text and xml files, JSON format, images and on and on and on, it feels like there is no format of data left which I haven't worked upon, opened up in exhaustion, cleaned and sanity checked with anticipation, loaded and transformed with a glimmer of hope in my eyes, and drawn insight from with a jumping up EUREKA moment when the output finally made sense!

So far, I have worked at the intersection of **ML, genomics, health informatics, AI, optimization, and full-stack product development**: from single-cell RNA-seq pipelines to LLM-powered clinical apps. And as the time goes on, I hope that I will add a whole new bunch of buzzwords here with my progressive work in this field! Until then, below is a more factual representation of my profile!
